Reasoning under uncertainty.
Authority never leaves the room.

AEGIS sits on a live incident bridge. It builds a picture of what has actually been established — from what people say and what telemetry measures — and interrupts, out loud, when a proposed action rests on something that isn't true.

Nobody talks to it. It has no code path to act on the systems being discussed. The only evidence it is working is that it stays quiet until the moment it shouldn't.

The problem

Interpretation is not authorization.
Authorization is not execution.

An incident is decided in a room, out loud, under pressure. A number gets recited from memory. A theory gets restated until it sounds settled. A destructive action gets proposed on top of it. None of those steps is a metric, so none of them is visible to a dashboard.

An AI that can read that room is useful. An AI that can act on it has quietly collapsed four different jobs into one. AEGIS holds them apart — and the last one it does not implement at all.

    AI interpretation

    The LLM converts messy speech into typed claims — and stops there.

    proposed_action · rollback · core-db

    Its response schema has no risk field. There is nowhere for it to put one.

    Four checks over typed fields produce the tier and the reasons behind it.

    HIGH · blast_radius_schema_break

    risk_engine imports no provider and holds no connection.

  3. 03 · human

    Human authorization

    An explicit utterance, attributed to a speaker, naming or answering the action.

    status: pending

    Silence, ambiguity and timeouts leave it pending forever. There is deliberately no timeout path into resolution.

  4. 04 · safety boundary

    Execution: Absent by design

    Not implemented, not stubbed, not behind a flag. Authority never leaves human hands.

How it thinks

Eight stages, and the model is only allowed to do one of them.

The LLM's entire job is the second row. Every risk decision below it is Python comparing typed fields, and every consequential step waits for a person. Each stage below carries the artifact it actually produces.

  1. 01

    Speech · conversation

    Agora relays the room. Every utterance enters as one transcript event carrying a turn id, so a retried delivery is the same turn rather than a second one.

    uid 1002 · final · voice

  2. 02

    Typed claims

    The only stage the model owns. Messy speech becomes typed claims: facts, hypotheses, proposed actions, decisions, confirmations, holds. Anything that does not validate is dropped rather than guessed at.

    {type: "hypothesis", metric_ref: "pool_utilization", value: 40}

  3. 03

    Beliefs

    Claims become durable state — theories with a status, facts with a speaker, a decision ledger, and pending actions that each record the hypothesis which justified them.

    action#7 justified_by hypothesis#a1

  4. 04

    Evidence

    Telemetry and submitted readings are the ground truth. A claimed number and a measured number stay separate objects and are never reconciled into one.

    claimed 40% · measured 91%

  5. 05

    Risk analysis

    Four deterministic checks: contradicted evidence, unconfirmed root cause, blast radius over the dependency graph, and reversal of a decision already taken. The tier and its reasons come out of comparisons, not out of a prompt.

    EVIDENCE_CONTRADICTION · HIGH

  6. 06

    Resolution

    A pure, total policy decides what a human utterance actually authorized. A named target decides it; a bare reply needs exactly one open action and has to be timely. It refuses rather than guesses.

    RESOLVED | AMBIGUOUS | UNRELATED

  7. 07

    Intervention governor

    One spoken intervention per 45 seconds on a monotonic clock, chosen by utility rather than arrival order, and packed exactly into a 512-byte speech budget.

    window closed · 31s remaining

  8. 08

    Human decision

    The terminal stage, and the only one that can authorize anything. Who decided it is recorded against the action; that attribution is the audit trail the product exists to produce.

    confirmed · by uid 1002

Belief retraction

Most systems append. This one can revise what a belief already caused.

Every proposed action records the hypothesis that justified it. When reality invalidates that theory, the pipeline walks the edge backwards and re-evaluates the unresolved actions still resting on it — because an action carrying a verdict computed against a belief nobody holds any more is the quiet failure this whole product is about.

Ambiguity

“Yeah” is not an authorization.

Confirmation attribution is a pure, total policy rather than a heuristic. A named target decides it. A bare reply needs exactly one open action and has to be timely, measured from the last moment that action was live in the room. Everything else is refused — and a non-resolved decision structurally cannot carry an action, because the dataclass raises.

      The intervention governor

      One channel. Forty-five seconds.
      Whatever matters most right now.

      Interrupting a human being out loud is the scarcest resource in the system, so it is scheduled rather than queued. Utility is tier plus independent findings, decayed on a 90-second half-life; the window takes the maximum. The scan is linear on purpose — utility is time-varying, which breaks the stable-key assumption a heap depends on.

          Exact 0/1 knapsack over the rendered cost of each sentence. The lead finding is mandatory; the rest compete for what is left. Greedy takes the first equal-tier finding and can then afford nothing.

                    What has not been verified

                    Live voice has never run end to end. Whether an interrupt priority actually cuts through a human mid-sentence, whether manual turn detection keeps the agent quiet, and whether speaker identities survive overlapping speech are all unobserved on a real channel. Our side of that contract — the client, the 512-byte cap, the speech composition — is covered by 31 tests against a mock transport, and the transcript relay that would feed it from a browser does not exist yet.

                    Disclosed here rather than in a footnote, because a landing page that quietly implies a capability is the same failure mode this system was built to catch.

                    Architecture

                    One process. One SQLite file. One in-process queue.

                    Deliberately a modular monolith. There is one incident, one room, and a requirement that a verdict is produced between two sentences of human speech — a network hop makes that worse, not more impressive. The interesting line in the diagram is not the boxes, it is where the state lock starts and stops.

There is a test whose only job is to grab the lock while a deliberately slow sink is mid-call; if it ever fails, someone has put speaking back under the lock and one unreachable voice service can freeze the entire incident.

                    Naming only what reads the changed schema understates the damage.                       Safety philosophy

                      AI interpretation ≠ deterministic authorization deterministic authorization ≠ human authorization human authorization ≠ execution

                      AEGIS holds all four apart on purpose. It reasons at the first boundary, computes at the second, waits at the third, and does not exist at the fourth. Everything below is a red line in the specification, and several have a test whose only job is to fail if someone simplifies them.

                      • 01No execution surface. The entire outbound vocabulary is join a call, say this, leave. There is no code path to act on the systems being discussed.
                      • 02No implicit authorization. Silence, ambiguity and timeouts leave an action pending forever. There is deliberately no timeout path into resolution.
                      • 03The model never decides risk. Its response schema has no field in which it could, and the risk engine imports no provider.
                      • 04At most one spoken intervention per 45 seconds, on a monotonic clock — with no exception for a genuine double-HIGH event.
                      • 05Speech is capped at 512 bytes, enforced before the request rather than trusted to the other end.
                      • 06No hypothesis is voiced as fact. Sentences are built from typed findings, never by interpolating claim text into an assertive template.
                      • 07Secrets never reach the frontend, a log, or version control. Error contexts carry status codes, never response bodies — a body can echo the request, and the request carries the header.
